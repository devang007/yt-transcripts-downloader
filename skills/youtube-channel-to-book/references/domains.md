# Domain Playbooks

Use at Phase 4 as a starting skeleton. Always let the ledger override the template — if the topic distribution doesn't match the skeleton, the ledger is right and the skeleton is wrong.

---

## Trading / markets channels

The dominant failure mode here is producing a book that reads like every other trading book. The value of a channel-derived book is precisely the parts that are *idiosyncratic* to this creator: his private vocabulary, his specific thresholds, the setups he named himself, the trades he says he refuses. Foreground those.

**Skeleton**

1. Who he is and what he claims to do — background, markets traded, timeframes, style, stated track record (as claims, cited)
2. His model of the market — what he thinks moves price and why most participants lose
3. Vocabulary — his terms defined, with standard-terminology translations in Editor's notes
4. Top-down analysis — the framework, higher timeframe to lower
5. Levels and structure — what he marks, when, and why
6. The setups — one section per named pattern: conditions, invalidation, what makes an A-grade vs B-grade instance
7. Entry mechanics — triggers, confirmation, what he'll chase and what he won't
8. Stops and invalidation — where and, more importantly, *why there*
9. Targets and exits — fixed targets, trailing, partials
10. Risk and position sizing — the arithmetic, exactly as stated
11. Trade management — after entry
12. The daily/weekly routine — prep, watchlist, journaling, review
13. Psychology — his stated failure modes and prescriptions
14. What he tells beginners — often scattered across many videos and highly valuable when consolidated
15. What he warns against — consolidated do-nots
16. Worked examples — end-to-end walkthroughs from his own trade recaps
17. Where the method is silent

**Extract with special care:** exact percentages and R multiples (with conditions); the difference between what he says and what he does in walkthroughs; instrument- and session-specific caveats; anything he's changed his mind about.

**Register:** report his rules as his rules. Front matter carries a plain educational-content-not-financial-advice note. Never add urgency, confidence, or profit framing the transcripts don't contain — and if his own claims about returns are prominent, the book reports them as claims he makes, with citations, and does not endorse them.

## Fitness / health / nutrition channels

**Skeleton:** principles → anatomy and terminology → programming logic → exercise library → progression and periodization → nutrition → recovery and sleep → common mistakes → population-specific guidance (beginners, older lifters, injuries) → sample programs built strictly from his own prescriptions.

**Care points:** dosages, protocols, and any supplement claims are `number` cards with full conditions. Where he cites research, note that he cites it, and don't extend the claim beyond what he said. Where advice could be unsafe for someone with a condition he doesn't discuss, an Editor's note saying "he addresses the general case; individual medical conditions are outside what these videos cover" is appropriate and honest.

## Business / startup / marketing channels

**Skeleton:** worldview and thesis → the model or framework → market/customer analysis → the playbook step by step → tools and stack → metrics he tracks → case studies from his own examples → scaling → failure modes → mindset.

**Care points:** business advice is heavily survivorship-biased and heavily conditional on era, market, and capital. Preserve dates aggressively — a 2019 acquisition playbook may be inapplicable now. Numbers (CAC, conversion rates, revenue) are claims about his own business, cited as such.

---

## Technical / tutorial / educational channels

**Skeleton:** foundations → core concepts in dependency order → the toolchain → step-by-step procedures → worked projects → debugging and troubleshooting → advanced topics → his opinions on practice and tooling.

**Care points:** version drift is the big one — a procedure from three years ago may not run today. Date every procedure and flag version dependencies. Code shown on screen is often absent or garbled in transcripts; extract the *approach* rather than reconstructing code from audio, and say so. Tier B is more generous here (a technical reader benefits from context), but the boundary rule is unchanged.

## Interview / podcast channels

Different structure entirely — the channel's value is the guests, not one creator's method.

**Skeleton:** organize by theme across guests, not by episode. Each chapter is a question the show returns to, with the range of answers different guests gave, points of consensus, and points of sharp disagreement. A "who's who" appendix maps guests to episodes.

**Care points:** attribution per speaker is critical and transcripts are frequently ambiguous about who's talking. If a segment's speaker can't be determined, don't guess — skip it. The host's own views are a separate thread worth tracking.
